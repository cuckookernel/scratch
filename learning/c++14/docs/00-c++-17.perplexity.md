C++17 is a solid target for interview prep, and it’s probably the **most widely used** C++ standard among developers surveyed in JetBrains’ ecosystem report. That said, I couldn’t verify a precise “percentage of companies” using it as their standard from the available source, because the data is framed as developer adoption rather than company policy. [blog.jetbrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2023/01/cpp-ecosystem-in-2022/)

## What the data says
JetBrains reported that C++17 was the most widely used standard in its 2022 ecosystem survey, with C++20 growing quickly behind it. The same report also says 53% of respondents already on C++17 planned to move to C++20. [blog.jetbrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2023/01/cpp-ecosystem-in-2022/)

## How to interpret that
For interviewing, you should treat C++17 as very relevant and worth knowing well, especially features like structured bindings, `std::optional`, `std::variant`, `if constexpr`, filesystem, and parallel algorithms. But because the available data doesn’t give a company-by-company adoption rate, it’s safer to say “widely used” rather than claim a specific company percentage. [blog.jetbrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2023/01/cpp-ecosystem-in-2022/)

## Practical advice
If you’re preparing for interviews, focus on C++17 first and only add C++20 basics if you have time. A lot of teams still use a mix of C++14/C++17 in real codebases, so being fluent in C++17 usually gives the best return. [blog.jetbrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/clion/2023/01/cpp-ecosystem-in-2022/)
